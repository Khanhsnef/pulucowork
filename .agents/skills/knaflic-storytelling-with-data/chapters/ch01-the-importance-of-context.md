# Chapter 1: The Importance of Context

## Core Idea
Success in data visualization does not start with data visualization, but with understanding the specific context: Who you are communicating to, What you need them to do, and How you will use data to show it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Exploratory vs. Explanatory Analysis**:
  - *Exploratory*: Opening 100 oysters to find 2 pearls (data discovery). Do not present 100 oysters to your audience.
  - *Explanatory*: Presenting the 2 pearls clearly with a specific message and recommendation.
- **The Context Triad (Who, What, How)**:
  - **WHO**: Specific target audience. Avoid "general internal stakeholders."
  - **WHAT**: The desired action or decision. Frame as an explicit recommendation.
  - **HOW**: The data and visual mechanism used to illustrate the point.
- **The 3-Minute Story**:
  - A concise summary of the problem, evidence, and proposed action if you only had 3 minutes with the decision-maker.
- **The Big Idea**:
  - A single sentence containing 3 components: (1) your unique point of view, (2) what is at stake, and (3) the explicit action required.
- **Storyboarding**:
  - A low-tech outline (sticky notes/whiteboard) planning the structure and visual flow before creating slides or charts.

## Key Takeaways
1. Always shift from exploratory analysis to explanatory communication before presenting.
2. Never present without a clear call to action or recommendation.
3. Use the Big Idea to ground your narrative before designing any slides.
4. Storyboard first on paper to save hours of unnecessary chart formatting.
